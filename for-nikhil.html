What you asked

Three things: does trading the index itself beat trading individual stocks; does trading the index intraday (in and out the same day) work; and you noticed the stock trades were being held on average four days or more, so you wanted that checked too.

What was run

• The index (NIFTY and BANKNIFTY, through their futures) held for several days, roll-aware.
• The index intraday — in and out the same day, on 1-, 5- and 15-minute bars.
• The 210-stock book — the same idea applied to individual stocks, held for a few days.

The results

• Index, held for days: 0 trades taken. Not one setup ever cleared its own cost — so there was nothing to trade.

• Index, intraday: 11 trades, and it lost (−3.81R after full costs). Small sample, but nothing good in it.

• 210 stocks, held ~4 days: 937 trades, +1.88R after full costs, average hold about four days. Positive — but small enough that it is statistically no different from zero yet.

What it means

If there is any edge here, it is in individual stocks held for a few days — not in the index, and not in intraday trading. The index gave nothing to trade; intraday lost; the stock book is mildly positive after real costs but not yet proven — it could still be luck. So the honest read is: keep testing the multi-day stock book, and leave the index and the intraday idea alone.
