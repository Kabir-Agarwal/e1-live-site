## Charges vs edge, and the size question — the honest answer

The exam's charge is **proportional** (12.4 bps of turnover), and `net_per_trade_R` is already net of it. Under a
proportional cost, **both the gross edge and the charge scale with position size**, so the charge's *fraction of
the edge is constant in size* — it does not rise or fall as the position grows. Consequently:

- **There is no size at which charges "fall under 1% or 5% of the edge"** under the exam's model — the ratio is
  fixed by the 12.4 bps rate, independent of size, and **the certified net R holds at every size** (R is
  scale-invariant). The certified R therefore does **not** degrade as size grows.
- The only size-dependent (regressive) effect is the **₹20/leg brokerage cap** (`money.py:17` comment,
  "brokerage 0.03%/leg cap Rs20"): once per-leg turnover exceeds ~₹66,667 (0.03% = ₹20), brokerage caps and the
  effective cost % **falls** with size — which would only make larger size *cheaper*, i.e. improve net R.

**Data gap (stated, not invented):** the exam's per-pattern records
(`EXAMS-REAL-STAGING.json → certified_patterns[i]`) carry `mean_net_R`, `fires`, `hit_lb`, `win_pct`,
`total_net_R` — but **no per-trade turnover (entry price × shares), no gross edge, and no fixed-vs-proportional
charge split.** So the exact turnover/size at which the ₹20/leg cap bites — and thus a precise "1% / 5% of edge"
crossover — is **not computable from the exam files**. Computing it would require the exam to also store per-trade
turnover and a fixed-charge component; it does not, so no number is given here rather than a fabricated one.
